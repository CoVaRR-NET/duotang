

Cases (and genomic sampling) have been limited, however the data available to date are consistent with current case levels still being low, involving primarily a combination PQ subvariants (descended from <u id='NB_1_8_1'>NB.1.8.1</u>,), with some <u id='XFG'>XFG</u> subvariants, plus some <u id='BA_3_2'>BA.3.2</u> subvariants at lower levels in clinically relevant cases. While there are PQ subvariants of interest (such as SA variants descended from <u id='PQ_2_10'>PQ.2.10</u> and RC variants) more data is needed to confirm a variant driving a new increase to come later in clinically significant cases.



Variants of current interest (due to their current/potential growth advantage, mutations of potential functional significance, or spread in other countries):

** <u id='NB_1_8_1'>NB.1.8.1</u> subvariants, including in particular SA variants (descended from <u id='PQ_2_10'>PQ.2.10</u>, <u id='PQ_2_1'>PQ.2.1</u> subvariants (including SH variants) and <u id='PQ_2_8_1'>PQ.2.8.1</u>, subvariants (includes RC variants)

** Subvariants of <u id='XFG'>XFG</u>, including <u id='XFG_1_1'>XFG.1.1</u>/<u id='XFG_1_1_1'>XFG.1.1.1</u>

** <u id='PE_1_4'>PE.1.4</u>

** <u id='BA_3_2'>BA.3.2</u> (including RE subvariants) of interest due in part to evidence it is disproportionately affecting children. Also, it has been less detected in clinical cases verses wastewater).



We continue to monitor for, but have not recently detected, any other highly divergent variants in Canada (“saltation” lineages with a sudden increase in number of mutations).
